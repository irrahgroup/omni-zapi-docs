This page describes how {projectName} delivers events to your endpoint and what happens when a delivery fails.

It applies to every webhook type — [channel](/en/webhooks/channel-webhooks), [template](/en/webhooks/template-webhooks) and [default](/en/webhooks/default-webhooks) — in both the default and the Z-API formats.

## Respond with 2xx within 5 seconds

A delivery succeeds when your endpoint responds with a `2xx` status within **5 seconds**.

Receive the event, store it and respond. Do the heavy processing later, outside the request. A slow response counts as a failure, and the event is sent again.

## When the event is sent again

| Your endpoint's response | What happens |
|---|---|
| `2xx` | Delivered. No retry. |
| No response within 5 s, connection error, `408`, `429` or `5xx` | Retry, following the table below. |
| Any other `4xx` | No retry: your endpoint rejected the event. |
| `3xx` | {projectName} follows the redirect. See [Redirects](#redirects). |

Each wait counts from the previous attempt:

| Attempt | Wait |
|---|---|
| 1st | immediate |
| 2nd | 1 minute |
| 3rd | 5 minutes |
| 4th | 30 minutes |
| 5th | 2 hours |
| 6th | 6 hours |
| 7th | 12 hours |

That is up to 7 attempts, within **23 hours** of the event. After that, the event is no longer sent.

<Note>
  If your endpoint keeps failing, {projectName} pauses deliveries to your address for a while and then resumes them. Events from that pause are not lost: they follow the same attempts, within the 23-hour window.
</Note>

## Redirects

{projectName} follows your endpoint's redirects, up to 10, and the response from the last address is the one that counts.

- On `301`, `302` and `303`, the next request is a `GET`, **without the body**. If the new address responds `2xx`, the delivery counts as delivered, but the event does not reach your system.
- On `307` and `308`, the next request repeats the `POST`, with the same body.
- When the redirect leads to another domain, the {projectName} HTTP client does not forward the `Authorization` header. So `BEARER` and `BASIC` authentication does not reach the new address.
- After 10 redirects, the attempt fails as a connection error and follows the retries.
- A `3xx` that does not lead to another address, such as a `304` or a response without the `Location` header, has no retry.

Register your endpoint's final URL, without redirects.

## Headers on each delivery

| Header | What it identifies |
|---|---|
| `x-webhook-event-id` | The event. It is the same on every webhook that receives this event. |
| `x-webhook-delivery-id` | The delivery of this event to this webhook. It is the same on every attempt, including resends from the [delivery center](/en/webhooks/deliveries). |
| `x-webhook-attempt` | The attempt number: `1`, `2`, `3`... |
| `x-idempotency-key` | The same value on every attempt. It is part of the [HMAC signature](/en/webhooks/signature). |

Each delivery also carries `content-type: application/json`, the authentication header configured on the webhook (see [Webhook authentication](/en/webhooks/introduction)) and this browser `user-agent`:

```
Mozilla/5.0 (X11; Linux x86_64) AppleWebKit/537.36 (KHTML, like Gecko) Chrome/145.0.0.0 Safari/537.36
```

If the webhook uses `CUSTOM_HEADER` authentication with the name `User-Agent`, the value you configured goes instead of this one.

If you use a WAF or an allowlist, allow this `user-agent`. Do not use the `user-agent` to tell whether the request came from {projectName}: use the [HMAC signature](/en/webhooks/signature).

## Deduplicate by `x-webhook-delivery-id`

The same delivery can arrive more than once. This happens, for example, when your endpoint processed the event but took more than 5 seconds to respond.

Keep the `x-webhook-delivery-id` after you process the event, for at least 24 hours, and discard repeats.

### Resends from the delivery center

A resend from the [delivery center](/en/webhooks/deliveries) arrives with the same `x-webhook-delivery-id` and the same `x-idempotency-key` as the original delivery, and with a new `x-webhook-attempt`.

- If you did not process the delivery, it is not among the stored ones, and the resend is processed normally.
- If you already processed the delivery and want to process it again, remove its `x-webhook-delivery-id` from the stored ones before you request the resend. Otherwise, the resend is discarded.

### In the `Z_API` format, one event can produce more than one delivery

Usually, each event becomes one delivery per webhook. In the `Z_API` format, the same event can become more than one delivery to the same webhook. Each delivery has its own `x-webhook-delivery-id`, and all of them have the same `x-webhook-event-id`.

Do not deduplicate by `x-webhook-event-id` alone: you lose deliveries.

## Arrival order is not guaranteed

Events are sent in the order they happen. But an event that failed is sent again minutes or hours later, while the following events keep being delivered. So a message's "read" status can arrive before the "delivered" status that failed on its first attempt.

- Order by the event time in the body (in the default format, the `moment` field), not by arrival time.
- For statuses, only move forward: ignore a status older than the one you already stored.

## When your endpoint is down

Events are not lost: they follow the attempts above for up to 23 hours. When your endpoint comes back, the delayed events arrive together with the new ones. Prepare your endpoint for a higher volume for a few minutes.

To recover what did not arrive within that window, use [Resend failed deliveries](/en/webhooks/resend-deliveries).

## Email notice when the webhook fails

When a webhook fails repeatedly, the workspace Owner, Administrators and Managers receive an email:

| Notice | When it is sent |
|---|---|
| 1st | After at least 3 consecutive failures, spanning at least 1 hour |
| 2nd | At least 1 hour after the 1st, if the webhook keeps failing, with 3 new failures |
| 3rd | At least 6 hours after the 2nd, if the webhook keeps failing, with 3 new failures |

- The email includes the webhook description, the URL with parts hidden, the number of failures and the last error. It never includes the secret, the credential or the delivery bodies.
- A successful delivery ends the sequence. If the webhook fails again, a new 1st notice is only sent 6 hours after the previous one.
- **The notice does not disable the webhook.** Deliveries continue, with the attempts described above.
