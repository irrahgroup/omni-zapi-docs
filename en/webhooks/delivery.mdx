This page describes how {projectName} delivers events to your endpoint and what happens when a delivery fails.

It applies to every webhook type — [channel](/en/webhooks/channel-webhooks), [template](/en/webhooks/template-webhooks) and [default](/en/webhooks/default-webhooks) — in both the default and the Z-API formats.

## Respond with 2xx within 5 seconds

A delivery succeeds when your endpoint responds with a `2xx` status within **5 seconds**.

Receive the event, store it and respond. Do the heavy processing later, outside the request. A slow response counts as a failure, and the event is sent again.

## When the event is sent again

| Your endpoint's response | What happens |
|---|---|
| `2xx` | Delivered. No retry. |
| No response within 5 s, connection error, `408`, `429` or `5xx` | Retry, following the table below. |
| Any other `4xx`, or `3xx` | No retry: your endpoint rejected the event. |

Each wait counts from the previous attempt:

| Attempt | Wait |
|---|---|
| 1st | immediate |
| 2nd | 1 minute |
| 3rd | 5 minutes |
| 4th | 30 minutes |
| 5th | 2 hours |
| 6th | 6 hours |
| 7th | 12 hours |

That is up to 7 attempts, within **23 hours** of the event. After that, the event is no longer sent.

<Note>
  If your endpoint keeps failing, {projectName} pauses deliveries to your address for a while and then resumes them. Events from that pause are not lost: they follow the same attempts, within the 23-hour window.
</Note>

## Headers on each delivery

| Header | What it identifies |
|---|---|
| `x-webhook-event-id` | The event. It is the same on every webhook that receives this event. |
| `x-webhook-delivery-id` | The delivery of this event to this webhook. It is the same on every attempt. |
| `x-webhook-attempt` | The attempt number: `1`, `2`, `3`... |
| `x-idempotency-key` | The same value on every attempt. It is part of the [HMAC signature](/en/webhooks/signature). |

## Deduplicate by `x-webhook-delivery-id`

The same delivery can arrive more than once. This happens, for example, when your endpoint processed the event but took more than 5 seconds to respond.

Keep the `x-webhook-delivery-id` values you already processed for at least 24 hours, and discard repeats.

## Arrival order is not guaranteed

Events are sent in the order they happen. But an event that failed is sent again minutes or hours later, while the following events keep being delivered. So a message's "read" status can arrive before the "delivered" status that failed on its first attempt.

- Order by the event time in the body (in the default format, the `moment` field), not by arrival time.
- For statuses, only move forward: ignore a status older than the one you already stored.

## When your endpoint is down

Events are not lost: they follow the attempts above for up to 23 hours. When your endpoint comes back, the delayed events arrive together with the new ones. Prepare your endpoint for a higher volume for a few minutes.

## Email notice when the webhook fails

When a webhook fails repeatedly, the workspace Owner and Managers receive an email:

| Notice | When it is sent |
|---|---|
| 1st | After at least 3 consecutive failures, spanning at least 1 hour |
| 2nd | At least 1 hour after the 1st, if the webhook keeps failing, with 3 new failures |
| 3rd | At least 6 hours after the 2nd, if the webhook keeps failing, with 3 new failures |

- The email includes the webhook description, the URL with parts hidden, the number of failures and the last error. It never includes the secret, the credential or the delivery bodies.
- A successful delivery ends the sequence. If the webhook fails again, a new 1st notice is only sent 6 hours after the previous one.
- **The notice does not disable the webhook.** Deliveries continue, with the attempts described above.
